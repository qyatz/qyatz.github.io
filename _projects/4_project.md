**柔らかい外装を有するヒューマノイドロボットの開発** (Development of a Humanoid Robot with a Soft Exterior)

## Overview

This project develops an endoskeleton-type humanoid robot covered with a soft exterior, aiming at a robot that can interact with people the way Baymax does.
Development is planned over several years:

| Year   | Goal                                              |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------- |
| FY2026 | Upper body                                        |
| FY2027 | Lower body                                        |
| FY2028 | Full-body integration and execution of real tasks |

This year's goal is to build an extendable and bendable torso and arms (the endoskeleton), the soft exterior, and the hands, integrate them into one robot, and exhibit it regularly at on-campus events and external exhibitions to confirm that it can interact with people.

## Motivation

Humanoid and companion robots such as ASIMO, Pepper and aibo have long shown the idea of robots that live alongside people and support daily life.
Although highly capable humanoids are now being developed, robots that genuinely coexist with people are still rare.
One reason is that robot hardware is still **hard**: when a robot shares space with people, the risk of injury or damage on contact with people and the environment must be reduced.

Making the hardware **soft** is an effective way to limit such damage, and robots with soft hardware — soft robots — are actively studied, including in our laboratory.
However, robots in which every element is soft tend to have low output or cannot support themselves.

## Approach

Like the human body, in which hard bones are surrounded by soft tissue, this project mounts a **soft exterior around a rigid skeleton**.
This aims to achieve the output and self-supporting skeleton that are difficult for fully soft robots, while reducing the risk of collisions.

For use in homes, the robot should also be compact: stored small when it is not needed, and deployed into a humanoid form when it is.
